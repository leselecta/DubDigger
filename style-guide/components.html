There is no cap on roles. Collapsing is what shortens a row: David Byrne's 101 stored credit strings across 258 shared releases come to 24 named roles, and all 24 show. A "+N more" was tried and removed, since a row that stops early is answering a question nobody asked.

Absence

No credits recorded. Nobody has entered credits for any of these 27 releases, so this is missing data rather than solo work.

Worked alone. Credits exist on 14 of 27 releases and name no one else. 

class="border-hairline-soft text-ink-muted max-w-[720px] border-b py-[18px]
       font-mono text-[0.8125rem] leading-relaxed"

Absence gets the same weight on the page as a list would. An empty result that looks like an answer is worse than an honest "no data here". The three states are different sentences and the coverage flags tell them apart: no releases at all, no credits entered, or genuinely solo.

    <!-- only when the row wants more than 390px -->
    <span class="scroll-hint pointer-events-none sticky right-0 flex w-16 shrink-0
                 items-center justify-end pb-4 {hint}"
          style="background: linear-gradient(to right, transparent, var(--color-bg) 70%)">

Tabs are links carrying a search param, never client state. The lists stay server rendered so a 556-row roster is never serialised into the page as JSON, every tab is a real URL, and it works before any JavaScript arrives.

A tab must not move the page. data-hold-scroll asks for that; the script that honours it lives in the layout. The mb-10 is the gap before the list starts: two hairlines close together read as one thick rule.

The row says when it scrolls, on the rows that scroll. A gradient and an arrow, held at the right edge by sticky, faded out over the last 40% of the travel by the scroll-hint utility. Scroll-driven CSS, so it costs no client budget, and it is a real flex item rather than an overlay so that where it stops it sits past the last tab rather than on top of it.

Which rows get one is arithmetic. A mono row's width is exact: 9.62px a character at 13px and 0.14em of tracking, a 24px gap between tabs, 48px of gutter. Fits 390 and there is no hint; otherwise it hides at the first 40px step above the row's own width. Three artist tabs want up to 530px, two label tabs 322px at the most, which is why the label page has no arrow at all.

The count stays one grey in both states. It used to take a dimmer shade when inactive, which asked the ramp for a step below ink-faint that does not exist, and said the same thing twice.

Never show a grade as a bare word where there is room to say what it stands on. Two different things put an artist on a step, and a page saying "medium" without saying which is claiming cluster work that may not exist. The clause after the comma is ink-faint, and a lifted grade always says it was lifted.

last is a prop, not a wrapping div. A <dl> may hold a <div> grouping a dt and a dd — and that is exactly the div this component already is, so wrapping it put a second one in between and the grouping stopped being one the parser recognises.

No page means no link. 43% of member relations, 66% of aliases and 69% of "member of" point at ids with no page. getRelations returns inCorpus and the chip drops the href without it, losing the hover and the pointer so nothing invites the click. The Ndagga Rhythm Force is the case that found it.

OutboundLinks

Discogs Wikipedia Bandcamp hardwax.com 
props: kind ("artist" | "label"), id, urls[]
Max 5 of the entity's own URLs, deduped by name, plus Discogs always first.
Unrecognised hosts keep their hostname rather than reading "Website".
http and https only — the urls column is contributor-entered and holds exotic schemes.

This is the cheap middle ground on images, not a substitute for them. The Discogs URL is pure id substitution, so it needs no API call and carries no licensing exposure. It is not "images are done".

The id forms are the interesting ones: they turn a bio into more threads to follow. Links inside prose use underline underline-offset-2 rather than link-rule, because a border-bottom inside a wrapping paragraph breaks at the line end.

The two sizes ask for the accent in opposite ways. The hero wears it from the start, since it is why the home page exists, and marks focus by doubling the edge with an inset shadow — a thicker border would eat its own padding box and shunt the text sideways on every click. The header one rests quiet on edge-strong and takes the accent only when reached for.

The magnifier is the submit button, not decoration. It fills the full height of the field so the target is the whole right end rather than an 18px glyph.

Three rows, then the way out. A dropdown is read at a glance while the hands are still on the keys, and past the third the ranking starts putting a name nobody typed under one they did. What does not fit belongs on the results page, which is one keystroke away and built for forty of them.

“View all results” is that keystroke written down. It is a real option in the listbox rather than a footer under it, so arrowing past the third name lands on it and Enter follows it, and the script needs no line for any of that. It only appears under names: a miss returns nothing and the dropdown stays shut, because “view all” of nothing offers to show emptiness at greater length. The chevron is the tab row’s, at the size the tab row draws it.

A suggestion row carries its grade, in the same five words and the same shades the results column uses. A shortlist that ranked names and said nothing about why would be ordering by a measure it kept to itself. The grade folds away below md — sr-only, not hidden, so the row is still read out with it.

The highlighted row takes the accent as a ground, like the current nav cell: it is the thing being reached for. The rows come from /suggest as markup, so the script only fetches, assigns and moves that highlight — 1,622 bytes, and the form underneath is the same plain GET with or without JavaScript.

The way out wears the accent at rest, the only place on the site it grounds something nobody is on yet. It earns it by being the offer the three names above it are not: visible without being read. It costs that row its highlight, since a row already accent cannot say the arrows are on it by turning accent, so hover and data-active invert to the ink ground Load more and the nav cells take.

Shell

The shell is drawn once, in Base.astro. Skip link, header, <main id="content">, footer, contact dialog. A page renders its own bands and nothing else. It used to render its own <SiteHeader /> too, which is how the site ended up with no <main> on it anywhere.

                          // become a band rather than a marker

A native <details>, so it announces its own state, works with the keyboard, and comes back closed after a router swap with no code. The script buys only the two frames the element does not give you: opening off-canvas, and staying open long enough to slide back.

The drawer wraps focus rather than hiding the page. No inert, no aria-hidden on the background. The page showing through the scrim is the point of a sheet over a panel, and marking it inert would hide the very thing the design keeps in view.

A modal hides the page and a sheet does not, and that is not a contradiction. A native <dialog> opened with showModal() gives the inertness, the focus trap, Escape and the focus return for free. Two overlays, two claims, two mechanisms.

autofocus is on the dialog, not on anything inside it. The default is the first focusable descendant, which put focus on the close button — and a touch browser rings that with the accent, as though closing were the offer.

LabelledBand

The idea

Every record is connected to another. That web of credits is how crate diggers actually find music, and it all sits in Discogs. 
class="border-hairline grid gap-6 border-t py-14 md:grid-cols-[180px_1fr]"
  h2 → class="mono-label text-ink text-[0.8125rem] md:mt-1"

The md:mt-1 is not a nudge, it is the leading. The label is 13px with the browser's own leading; body copy is 17px at 1.6, and that leading is split above and below every line, so the prose starts about 4px lower. Below md the grid is one column and there is nothing to align to.

The heading overrides mono-label to ink at 0.8125rem. Band prose is ink-muted, which is the grey mono-label already carries. Side by side that reads as one system; stacked on a phone it is a caption above prose rather than a title over it. The other mono-label users caption a value and stay muted at 0.75rem. The size is the top of the structural range, not a new one.

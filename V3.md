# V3 backlog

Parked work, with the reasoning that got it parked. Nothing here is scheduled.

**This was `V2.md` until 2026-09-09.** v2 was re-scoped that day to one thing,
the release page, which is what it actually built and is nearly done shipping.
Everything that had accumulated under the v2 name moved here rather than holding
the release page hostage to a backlog it has nothing to do with. The rename is
the whole of the change: not one line of reasoning below was revisited, and the
items are parked on the same grounds as before.

The rule from CLAUDE.md still holds: each item is a later evolution of a working
core, and if a v1 task seems to need one, stop and flag it rather than building
it.

## Companies as entities: distributors, shops, pressing plants, studios

Parked 2026-09-01, after Hard Wax came up as a missing page.

### The gap

Hard Wax is Discogs label `296851`, "Record store and distribution company based
in Berlin-Kreuzberg, Germany. Founded by Mark Ernestus in 1989." It has no page
on Dub Digger, and no dial reaches it.

`label_artist_pairs` holds 0 rows for it. That table is built in `pass1-seed.ts`
from every release's `<labels>` field across the whole dump, and covers 963,557
distinct label ids. So Hard Wax never appears in a `<labels>` field on any of the
19.3M releases. Its entire presence is company credits, `Distributed By` and the
like, and `release-stream.ts` never reads `<companies>`. `schema.sql` says so on
line 3: companies and identifiers are discarded at parse time.

This is structural, not a threshold that needs moving. Both channels of pass 2
need a `<labels>` ref or a credit, and a distributor has neither.

Measured on the 5,000-release sample (head of the dump, so biased, and indicative
of shape only): 17,958 company refs, 4,097 distinct company entities, of which
2,335 (57%) never appear in a `<labels>` field anywhere in the full dump. The
roles that skew most invisible are `Produced At` 87%, `Designed At` 82%,
`Recorded At` 80%, `Mixed At` 79%, `Published By` 54%. `Distributed By` is only
20% invisible, which is what makes Hard Wax unusual: most distributors do double
duty as labels, and it does not.

### Four constraints any v2 design has to satisfy

These came out of investigating what a graded Hard Wax page would do. They are
constraints, not preferences, and each one has a failure behind it.

1. **It cannot write to `seed_labels`.** `very high` IS the seed-label rule
   (`config.ts`, and `check-corpus` asserts the two counts match by name), so any
   entity reaching the top step becomes a seed label and opens channel B. Channel
   B has no crowding guard: `pass2-expand.ts` has a bare `if (channelB)` against
   channel A's `if (channelA && !crowded)`. A distributor's roster is shelf
   proximity by definition, which is the exact failure `channelAMaxPeopleToAdmit`
   exists to prevent, one layer up and with no dial on it.

2. **It cannot share `label_artist_pairs`.** That table is the grade's
   denominator. In the sample, EFA appears once as a label ref and 175 times as a
   distributor, PIAS France 7 against 86, PIAS UK 1 against 25, Submerge 1 against
   22. Merging companies in would stop Kompakt being graded on the 210 acts it
   released and start grading it on everything its distribution arm shipped, so a
   change aimed at a shop would regrade labels that are currently correct.

3. **It cannot use `label_coverage.relevance`.** Simulating the seed-label gate
   over distribution in the sample, 52 of 139 distributors with 5+ releases clear
   `very high`, including DADC, PMDC, Eurodisc Manufacturing, Warner Music
   Manufacturing Europe and BOD Berlin Optical Disc. Those denominators come from
   5,000 releases, so a major's plant looks like a boutique and the number itself
   is not trustworthy. What it does show is real: a plant and a scene label press
   the same records, so the ratio cannot separate them. Only `entity_type_name`
   can, and that is an uncontrolled field with 25+ values.

4. **It needs its own vocabulary.** The label page reason line reads
   `X% of everyone it released is in the dub techno cluster`. For a distributor
   the true sentence is "of everyone it shelved", and the corpus rule already says
   shelving is not membership. No rewording repairs that, because the measure
   underneath is the wrong measure. Such an entity should carry no relevance grade
   at all.

So if this ships, it ships as an ungraded entity of a different kind, in its own
table, never feeding the corpus boundary.

### What was rejected, and why

**A hand-written cross-reference on the orbit's label pages.** Structurally it
would have been fine, and `web/src/lib/roles.ts` is the precedent for a
hand-written table read at display time. It fails on honesty instead: six label
pages carrying a distributor line and 114,220 carrying nothing says those 114,220
had no distributor, which is the rule against rendering an empty result that looks
like a positive answer. It is also unfalsifiable from the published file, since
the data that would check it was discarded at parse time. Same defect as
`Dubplates & Mastering` grading `very high` off five releases where somebody typed
a mastering room into the label field.

### Cost when it is picked up

The releases dump is deleted (only `artists` and `labels` remain on disk), so any
version of this needs the 10.4 GB re-download plus a full pass 1 and pass 2.

## Label relations: parent labels and sublabels

Separate from the above, and cheap. Raised in the same investigation and worth
doing on its own merit rather than as a patch for Hard Wax.

The labels dump carries relations that are currently dropped. `entity-stream.ts`
reads id, name, profile and urls for a label, and skips the rest:

```xml
<label><id>173661</id><name>Wax Treatment</name>
  <parentLabel id="296851">Hard Wax (2)</parentLabel>
```

In the dump: 292,940 parent relations across 63,351 distinct parents, and 308,335
sublabel relations. Of those targets, 25% of parents and 9% of sublabels have a
page in the corpus, which is the same shape as the artist relations already
shipping (aliases 34%, members 57%, groups 31%) and calls for the same treatment.
`getRelations` returns `inCorpus`, and `Chip` drops the href without it, so the
corpus can name a label it never admitted without pivoting anyone into a 404.

Cost is low. `entities` reads only the artists and labels dumps, both still on
disk, so this is `entities`, `derive` and `publish` plus a chip row on the label
page. No releases dump, no pass 1, no pass 2. Restricted to the 114,226 corpus
labels it is on the order of tens of thousands of rows, against the 918,040
already in `artist_relations`.

The catch, and the reason it does not close the Hard Wax gap on its own: it puts
Hard Wax on exactly one page, Wax Treatment's, because that is the only sublabel
the dump records for it. Discogs does not record Basic Channel or Chain Reaction
as sublabels of the shop.

## A release page: the third entity

Raised 2026-09-06. Explored, not decided, and nothing was built.

### The gap

Every release title on an artist or label page already links straight out to
Discogs, `artist/[id].astro:348` and `label/[id].astro:170`. That is 1,095,302
links leaving the site to reach a thing the corpus already holds. The release is
also the object a collaboration is actually made of: artist pivots to label and
label pivots to artist, while the record where the two met has nowhere to live.

### It needs no API, and that is the whole reason it is cheap

The published file already carries, per release: title, year and provenance from
`releases`; the full artist line with join phrases from `release_artists`
(1,261,347 rows); every credit with its raw role string from `release_credits`
(4,110,875); and label plus catalogue number from `release_labels` (1,144,364).
`creditLine()` in `web/src/lib/roles.ts` already names those roles, so the page
is a route and a query against tables that are already there.

What is missing, and what each would cost:

- **Tracklist.** Never parsed. `release-stream.ts` skips the whole subtree so
  track-level credits cannot be mistaken for release-level ones. Recovering it
  means the 10.4 GB re-download and a full pass 2.
- **Styles and genres.** In the ingest database, dropped at publish. Republishing
  them is an edit to the raw-table list in `publish.ts` and a larger web file. No
  re-parse.
- **Format, country, notes.** Discarded at projection, by the same rule.
- **Images.** Restricted Data. Out for v1 and out here.

**A live API top-up was considered and rejected in the same conversation.** It is
the second path from the deferred-images section arriving early under a different
name: a live external dependency, a 60 request per minute ceiling, mandatory
attribution, new failure modes, and the natural on-ramp for "while we are calling
anyway". It breaks the one property the architecture is built on, that the server
reads one static file and nothing else. Local only, or not at all.

### Thin pages are accepted

Measured on the published file: 443,005 of 1,095,302 releases carry no credits at
all (40%), 69,907 have no label, and 32,832 have no year. The worst page is a
title, a nothing, and a link out.

**Simone took this deliberately on 2026-09-06.** If Discogs holds a release page,
that is data, and a thinner page is still a page. So every corpus release gets
one, rather than minting pages only where there is something to say, which would
have put two different link behaviours on one list. The existing honesty rule
covers the wording: "No credits recorded" has to stay visibly distinct from
worked solo, the same as on an artist page.

### What it has to satisfy

1. **It is a third entity, and the scope section says two.** A release is not a
   track, and tracks staying out is untouched by this. But "Two entities: Artist
   and Label" becomes false the day this ships, so that line gets edited then,
   not quietly outgrown.
2. **It carries no relevance grade.** The five steps measure an artist's or a
   label's distance from the cluster. A single record is not a body of work and
   has no ratio behind it. `is_seed` is a fact about how it entered the corpus,
   not a grade, and must not be shown as one.
3. **It stays out of the sitemap.** 1,095,302 URLs against today's 1,808. The
   rule already holds for the other half million artist pages: the sitemap lists
   the core, and the rest are reachable by link.
4. **Names with no page are chips, not links.** A crowded record is where this
   bites hardest, and it is also the best argument for the page: a 34-name Ndagga
   session finally has somewhere to show all 34, and `channelAMaxPeopleToAdmit`
   means many of them have no page. Same treatment as relations, `inCorpus` and
   `Chip` dropping the href.
5. **The CTA is pure id substitution.** `https://www.discogs.com/release/<id>`,
   the `OutboundLinks` precedent, no licensing exposure.

### Saving is not part of this

It was the example that prompted the question and it is explicitly a later use.
Per Gall's Law the page is the working simple system: a route and a query against
a read-only file. Saving needs accounts and writable state against a database
whose entire premise is that it is static, which is a separate decision and a
much larger one. Do not let it justify skipping the design of the page.
